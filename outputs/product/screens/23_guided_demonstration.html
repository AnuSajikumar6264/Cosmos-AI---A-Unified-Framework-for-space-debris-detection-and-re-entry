What COSMOS-AI is. An evidence-aware, read-only satellite digital twin: it reads a dated catalogue snapshot and archived evaluations of orbital lifetime, re-entry, telemetry anomaly detection, conjunction risk and debris imagery, keeps every value tied to its artifact digest and run, states uncertainty and abstention explicitly, and ranks objects for attention with a rule it can explain. What it addresses: turning heterogeneous space-situational evidence into traceable mission-level assessments without pretending that unlinked evidence is linked. What it is not: no live telemetry, no on-demand model inference, no operational control, no spacecraft health verdict. Each stage below links to the real screen and states what you will see, what it means and its limits.

Stage 4 — Orbital intelligence

Do: open NORAD 37607; read the identity panel, the orbital elements and the "Orbital lifetime assessment". You see: mean elements (perigee, apogee, inclination; no state vector), the 5–95 % lifetime interval, its relation to the 30-day horizon, the per-horizon probabilities, the coherence check, and the subgroup-coverage cue for design-derived area-to-mass objects (origin 2016, 80 % nominal, 0.5625 observed). Means: the interval is a Mondrian conformal interval from the archived lifetime expert; the horizon cue is display only. Limit: 123 records are excluded as non-coherent (L17); quantiles at the cap mean "at least this long"; coverage under later space weather is not guaranteed.

Stage 5 — Re-entry prediction

Do: same page, "Re-entry assessment"; then open NORAD 25544 and NORAD 12. You see: p(30 d / 90 d / 1 yr), the Monte-Carlo time quantiles and their horizon relation; for the ISS an explicit operator-controlled abstention with the base rate and no interval; for VANGUARD R/B quantiles censored at the 1826.25-day cap; the variant table shows the physics-prior, history-posterior and lifetime-comparison variants side by side. Means: the reported forecast is one variant; disagreement between variants (up to 10.6× in the archived decomposition, X7) is shown, not hidden. Limit: the dated forecast was evaluated on the non-blind T1 window only; prospective validation is blocked until a later snapshot exists.

Stage 6 — Satellite health and telemetry

Do: open Telemetry Benchmarks, then a channel. You see: 3 prepared anonymised ESA-ADB missions, labelled intervals, 128 benchmark arms with event-wise F0.5, precision, recall and false alarms, and the methodology block (pre-registered alarm-layer study, K6). Means: alarm-layer selection dominated scorer capacity; the AR(8) arm beat PatchTST, TCN and Chronos on the official metric. Limit: no mission can be matched to a NORAD id, so no catalogued object has telemetry and no spacecraft health is inferred — the object page says "Spacecraft health — not determinable".

Stage 7 — Collision and conjunction analysis

Do: open Risk Intelligence. You see: 2,167 held-out Kelvins CAC events, the distribution of predicted log10 collision probability, the 1e-6 screening threshold, 5–95 % quantiles, the methodology block and the C4 abstention panel. Means: a benchmark quantile-regression result; the reliability gate's weight is near zero (a negative, externally anchored result). Limit: events are anonymised; nothing is an operational warning; priority is never derived from a physical probability.

Stage 8 — Debris and optical imagery

Do: open Debris Intelligence, then a sequence and a frame. You see: SpotGEO test sequences with the classical front end's hard detections overlaid on the real frame; the detector benchmark table (best archived F1 0.765 for resnet18_pretrained; served detector F1 0.084); the front-end ablation finding. Means: learned detectors score about 0.75 official F1; the classical front end whose detections are shown scores 0.084. Limit: pixel positions without astrometric calibration, confidence or time cannot be associated with any catalogued object.

Stage 9 — Decision support

Do: on NORAD 37607 read "Decision priority"; then open the run's ranking. You see: rank 2 of 31396, score 2, the recorded inputs (p₃₀, mass, high-consequence flag), the formula exactly as the manifest recorded it, the C4 health-escalation abstention, and the interval-versus-horizon cue. Means: the score is p_reentry_30d × (2 if mass ≥ 500 kg else 1): an operational ordering, not a probability and not a risk estimate. Limit: the rule uses the point probability, not the interval; its effectiveness against outcomes is not established (no outcome window has been scored yet).

Stage 10 — Digital twin and provenance

Do: open Twin State, switch the policy to archived_only, and use the policy links on an object page. You see: RE-EMITTED · twin_state/1.1 · verified; 31,396 records compared, 31,396 equal, 0 different; 31,396 valid records, 0 duplicates; not-compared fields listed by reason; recorded and computed SHA-256, transformation digest, schema version, model version UNAVAILABLE. Means: the re-emitted record is value-equivalent to the archive and to the catalogue chain; representation differences (operator-controlled abstention) are named, not forced equal. Limit: re-emission regenerated nothing; no fitted model is persisted; the archived record carries no per-output provenance.

Stage 11 — Scenario analysis

Means: the real pipeline ran over saved artifacts into a new directory; the result is reproducible from its configuration digest. Limit: no scenario retrains, rescores or alters a scientific assumption; historical runs are never modified.

Stage 12 — Pipeline and research evidence

Do: open Run Explorer (91 runs), the latest run page and a comparison of two runs. You see: module statuses with lineage (code, configuration, data artifacts, model version), 8 output tables with downloads, the raw manifest, and the value-level comparison (identical tables by digest, differences by key). Means: every displayed number is traceable to an artifact digest, a run id and a code revision. Limit: model version is unavailable throughout because no fitted model is persisted.

Stage 13 — Final mission summary

Established. A pre-registered telemetry study (K6) showing that alarm-layer selection dominates scorer capacity, with a negative deep-learning finding; a catalogue-scale, censoring-aware lifetime benchmark (K7) measuring that model selection does not transfer across temporal origins; an evidence-contracted twin whose integration premise (K1) was tested and not supported, and whose OPS-SAT fusion claim (K8) was withdrawn; a versioned twin state verified equivalent to its archive and to the runtime chain.

Outside the validated scope. Live data, on-demand inference, spacecraft health states, collision warnings for named objects, debris-to-object association, prospective forecast validation (pending a snapshot dated ≥ 2026-11-26), cross-satellite generalisation, and the effectiveness of the priority ranking against outcomes. Prospective evaluation is currently BLOCKED.
