Why there is no power, thermal or attitude panel. ESA-ADB publishes its channels and subsystems anonymised (channel_1, subsystem_1, …), with no units and no physical meaning attached. A power, thermal or ADCS view would require inventing that mapping, so this console does not offer one: it serves the real series, their ground-truth intervals, and the benchmark results of the alarm-layer study instead.
Methodology, dataset provenance and limitations

Dataset. ESA Anomaly Dataset (ESA-ADB) v1.0, Missions 1–3, published anonymised by ESA; prepared arrays under data/feature1_health/esa_ad/prepared/ (manifest data/feature1_health/esa_adb/MANIFEST.json). The official train/test split is respected; the benchmark's labelled intervals are ground truth, not detections.

Method under study (K6, pre-registered). A per-channel AR(8) forecaster scores residuals; the alarm layer (conformal threshold with Benjamini–Hochberg control, event merging) is selected on the training half and frozen before the test half is scored. Baselines: PatchTST, TCN and zero-shot Chronos-Bolt with the same alarm layer. Metrics: event-wise F0.5 (the official metric, precision-weighted), precision, recall, false alarms per year and ADTQC. Pre-registration freeze research_design/preregistration/freeze.json; confirmatory record outputs/benchmarks/telemetry_esa_adb/oof_protocol/CONFIRMATORY.json.

Finding. Alarm-layer selection dominates scorer capacity: the AR(8) arm reaches event-wise F0.5 0.074 on Mission 1 against 0.048 (PatchTST), 0.030 (TCN) and 0.024 (Chronos); Mission 2 0.962 against 0.952. The exploratory X4 cells attribute about 0.30 of the 0.353 selection gain to the selection data itself.

Limitations. Anonymisation prevents any join to a NORAD object, so no catalogued satellite has telemetry here and no spacecraft health state is inferred; several arms are single-seed; a benchmark result is not operational validation; cross-satellite generalisation is not established (one spacecraft per mission).

How to read these numbers

Each row is a detection-performance measurement of a benchmark arm on the ESA-ADB test half — not a per-object anomaly probability, and not a health score for any spacecraft.

Event-wise F0.5 weights precision above recall, following the official benchmark metric. Some arms are single-seed, so differences between nearby rows should not be read as robust.

These are benchmark results. They are not independent operational validation.
