Objectives

What the project set
out to establish

Five objectives, each with the artifact that settles whether it was met. Three were met as stated, one produced a negative answer that is reported as such, and one is still open because it needs a future catalogue snapshot.

  1. O1 — Build a catalogue-scale, censoring-aware evaluation protocol. A cohort of 64,516 objects with three forward-in-time origins (2006 sealed, 2011, 2016), launch-family clustering, and feature-free reference predictors that bound trivial performance. Met 64,516 objects3 origins
  2. O2 — Compare drag physics, learned models and their fusion like for like. A physics expert, seven learned quantile regressors and three fusion rules on identical splits with identical metrics. Met Fusion not superior
  3. O3 — Measure what the standard model-selection rule costs. Regret of “pick the lowest tuning loss” expressed in the published loss, with a rank inversion reproduced at all three origins. Met +18.7 % at 2016
  4. O4 — Quantify the uncertainty, and locate where it breaks. Mondrian conformalised quantile intervals, with coverage decomposed by subgroup until the failure was localised. Met, descriptively 56.3 % subgroup coverage
  5. O5 — Deliver a usable, auditable artefact. A dated digital twin of 31,396 objects and a read-only console in which every number traces to a hashed file, incoherent probabilities trigger abstention, and staleness is stated on every screen. Met Blind validation pending

What the project deliberately does not claim

No constituent algorithm is new: King-Hele drag theory, NRLMSIS 2.1, the NASA breakup-model area-to-mass distribution, gradient boosting, TabM, TabICL, sigmoid-gated combination, split conformal prediction and Mondrian calibration are all existing methods used as published. No arm is claimed to be state of the art against external published methods, because none could be re-implemented on this cohort without element-set data. The twin performs no live monitoring and no request-time inference.
